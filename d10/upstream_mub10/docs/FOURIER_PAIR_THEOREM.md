## 1. Setting

A vector is unbiased to I and F_10 iff, after normalising x_0 = 1, it is unimodular and satisfies
g_k(x) := Σ_j x_{j+k} conj(x_j) = 0 for k = 1..9. [proved: |F x|² is the Fourier transform of the periodic
autocorrelation]

For unimodular x we have conj(x_j) = 1/x_j. So these vectors are exactly the unimodular points of the Laurent system
g_k(x) = Σ_j x_{j+k}/x_j = 0, k = 1..9, with x ∈ (C*)^9 (x_0 = 1).

That system is in bijection with the cyclic 10-roots system via z_j = x_{j+1}/x_j. [proved]

## 2. Main theorem

**Hypothesis (H)** [cited: Faugère, via Führ-Rzeszotnik arXiv:1506.06738, table]: the cyclic-10 system has at most
34,940 solutions in C^10. (H) is used only as an upper bound on the number of distinct solutions. So it does not
matter whether 34,940 counts roots with or without multiplicity: we exhibit 34,940 distinct roots, all simple.

**Theorem A** [computer-assisted: rests on (H), on Arb ball arithmetic (python-flint 0.9), and on the scripts in §6].
1. The cyclic-10 system has exactly 34,940 solutions. All are nonsingular, each lies in an explicit certified box, and
   they form 49 orbits under the symmetry group (order 1600).
2. Exactly 3,040 normalised vectors are unbiased to I and F_10.
   - They form 10 orbits under the order-1600 group generated by: cyclic shift, modulation, index dilation by units,
     complex conjugation, and the Fourier transform.
   - Orbit sizes: 800, 400, 400, 400, 400, 200, 200, 200, 20, 20.
3. Every orthonormal basis B unbiased to I and F_10 is one of 1,136 explicit 10-subsets of these vectors.
4. For each such B, no vector of C^10 is unbiased to all of I, F_10 and B. So every MU triple containing {I, F_10} is
   strongly unextendible.
5. Hence no four MUBs in C^10 contain {I, F_10}.

**Unconditional variant (without (H))** [computer-assisted].
- The BKK bound states that the number of isolated roots in (C*)^10, counted with multiplicity, is at most the mixed
  volume. The mixed volume of cyclic-10 is 35,940 [cited by the orchestrator and recalled from the literature; not
  recomputed here]. BKK is an upper bound for any coefficients.
- Since we certified 34,940 distinct simple roots, there are **at most 1,000 further isolated roots**.
- BKK does not rule out positive-dimensional components. (H) also asserts that there are none.

**Theorem B** [computer-assisted: exact arithmetic in Q(ζ_20), with no intervals and no (H)].
- The 96 candidate bases in basis orbits 12–18 (orbit sizes 40, 40, 2, 8, 2, 2, 2) are exactly orthonormal bases
  unbiased to I and F_10.
- Every entry is ζ_20^a or ζ_20^a·u^{±1}, with u = (−√5 + 2i)/3.
- The proof checks one representative per orbit exactly in Q[t]/Φ_20(t), using √5 = 1 + 2(t^4 + t^16), i = t^5 and
  conj(t) = t^19. The checks are: |x_j|² = 1; g_k = 0 for all k; pairwise orthogonality. The symmetry group, whose
  maps are exact, then covers each whole orbit.
- So: **96 ≤ #(bases) ≤ 1,136**. With (H), exactly 1,136 if all candidates are bases.

**Statement C** [numerical].
- All 1,136 candidates are bases. Their inner products have Arb enclosures of radius about 1e-40 that contain 0.
- The remaining 1,040 candidates, in basis orbits 0–11, are built from vector orbits 0–6. No exact form for those
  vectors is known yet.

## 3. Proof architecture

1. **Hand step.** The reduction in §1, and the symmetry σ(x) = 1/conj(x) of the Laurent system:
   g_k(σx) = conj(g_{−k}(x)).
2. **Finding roots** (heuristic; it does not need to be rigorous). Damped Newton from random starts, in x- and
   log-coordinates, then closure under the group. Result: 34,940 roots in 49 orbits.
3. **Krawczyk test** (Arb, 106 bits, box radius 1e-9·max(1,|x_j|)). All 34,940 pass, so each box holds a unique root,
   which lies in the Krawczyk image K.
   - The boxes are pairwise disjoint: the minimum Chebyshev distance between centres is 0.132, while box radii are at
     most 6.2e-8.
   - So there are at least 34,940 distinct roots. With (H), these are all the roots.
4. **Unimodular test.** If σ(K) ⊂ X (the uniqueness box), then σ(root) is a root in X, so σ(root) = root and
   |x_j| = 1 exactly. This holds for 3,040 roots, re-certified at 256 bits with radius 1e-45.
   - For the other 31,900 roots, some |K_j| excludes 1.
5. **Pair tests.** All 3040² inner products were enclosed in Arb.
   - The "possibly orthogonal" pairs (enclosure of <u,v> contains 0) number exactly 57,680.
   - All maximal cliques of this graph were computed exactly (networkx): 1,136 of size 10 and 17,700 of size 2.
   - For every candidate basis B and every vector v, some u ∈ B has a certified |<u,v>|² ≠ 10. This gives items 4–5
     of Theorem A.
6. **Independent checks.** The orchestrator reports two independent Sonnet re-implementations. They reproduce:
   - the 34,940 certified roots;
   - the 3,040 / 31,900 split;
   - 57,680 edges and the same 1,136 cliques;
   - minimum bias defect 4.17807.

msolve modular F4 (GF(65521)) was stopped at step 15 by the 20-minute cap. It is a consistency check only, since a
modular degree is not an upper bound over Q.

**Classification** [numerical invariants plus exact symmetries].
- The invariant is the multiset of the 1,000 triple products √10³·<a,b><b,c><c,a>, symmetrised under conjugation. It is
  invariant under global unitaries, rephasing and permutation within bases, permutation of the three bases, and
  conjugation.
- It takes **17 distinct values**. Orbit pairs 14/16 and 17/18 coincide; everything else is separated.
- The group gives at most 19 classes.
- So the number of equivalence classes of MU triples containing {I, F_10} is **17, 18 or 19**. I did not try to
  build explicit equivalences for the pairs 14~16 and 17~18.
- Without conjugation the invariant takes 23 values. It is then invariant only under even permutations of the bases,
  so this is not a clean bound.

**Hadamard-matrix facts** relevant to a novelty check (all [numerical]):
- Isolated complex Hadamard matrices (defect 0) of order 10 occur as H_FB in orbits 0, 6, 9 and 13, and as H_IB in
  orbits 4 and 8. Their Haagerup-set sizes are 251, 134, 134, 10, 109 and 109.
- The catalogue lists isolated order-10 matrices (e.g. S_10, N_10A, X_10). These should be compared, since equal
  invariants would suggest equivalence. **Not done.**
- The 1,120 non-product bases (orbits 0–13) are neither tensor products nor eigenbases of any Weyl operator.

## 5. Plan: extending to the whole affine family

- **Domain.**
  - T⁴ ∋ (p1..p4), reduced by conjugation (p → −p), by the action of (Z/5)* permuting (p1..p4) (dilation of the C^5
    factor), and by p-translations absorbed into rephasings. Expect a fundamental domain of about 1/8 of T⁴. This must
    be confirmed by computing which (p) give equivalent pairs.
- **How the count changes** [inference].
  - For fixed p the unbiased vectors are the nonsingular zeros of a square real 9-phase system. By the implicit
    function theorem they move smoothly and keep their number until the real Jacobian becomes singular.
  - At such walls, pairs of unimodular roots meet and leave the torus as σ-paired non-unimodular roots.
  - The drop from 3,040 at the centre to about 1,500–1,850 at random points means walls separate the centre from
    generic parameters. The centre is not generic in this family, since it is highly symmetric. Many centre vectors
    survive on small neighbourhoods, because all 3,040 are nonsingular.
  - First step: track the 3,040 centre vectors along rays from p = 0 with a certified predictor-corrector, and record
    where each meets a wall. This maps the chamber structure.
- **Rigorous cover** (in the style of Jaming–Matolcsi–Móra–Szöllősi–Weiner, d = 6).
  - For each parameter box P, do three things:
    1. enclose all unbiased vectors, by a parametric Krawczyk test on each tracked branch, plus an exclusion test on
       the rest of T⁹ (the hard part);
    2. recompute the possibly-orthogonal graph;
    3. verify the clique-cover inequality.
  - Margins are about 4e-3 (smallest non-orthogonal |<u,v>|) and above 4 (bias defect). The phases have parameter
    sensitivity of order 1–10, so boxes need a width of about 1e-3.
  - That suggests up to about 10^12 boxes in 4 dimensions before adaptivity and symmetry, and up to about 10^10 after.
    This is infeasible on a 16-core laptop plus an 8 GB GPU, at roughly 10^5–10^6 boxes per second for the cheap
    tests.
  - What would make it feasible is replacing the T⁹ exclusion with algebra: count complex roots for p in an
    algebraic parametrisation (u_i = e^{ip_i}), using a parametric Gröbner basis or a monodromy-certified count, and
    use the cover only for the orthogonality step.
- **Realistic near-term target:** a rigorous statement on an explicit neighbourhood of p = 0. Every centre vector
  persists there, and no new vectors enter while no wall is crossed. This takes about 1 GPU-hour.

  - `analyse.py`: orbits, graph, bases, MU pairs

  - `cyc10_d.npy`: all 34,940 roots (x_1..x_9). `c10_cert.json`: certificate per root.
  - `bu10_x.npy`, `bu10_phases.npy`: the 3,040 vectors. The row is the vector id used everywhere.
  - `bu10hp_unimod.txt`: id, then Re/Im of x_1..x_9 to 40 digits (radius ≤ 1e-45).
  - `d10_analysis.json`: bases as index lists; orbit_of / orbit_reps; basis_orbit / basis_orbit_reps; ext_counts.
  - `d10_rigor.log` / `.json`.
  - `d10_triple_classes.json`.
  - `d10_basis_identification.txt`.
  - `affine.log`.
- **Exact forms** (`exactforms/`): `cyclotomic_bases.json` (exact entries (a, e) for the 7 representative bases),
  `orbit*_hp.json` (320-digit representatives).
- **Commands** (from `exact/`):
  1. `python3 code/cyclic.py 10 40000 1 data/cyc10_a.npy`
  2. `python3 code/cyclic.py 10 60000 3 data/cyc10_b.npy data/cyc10_a.npy`
  3. `python3 code/lognewton.py data/cyc10_b.npy 12 6 3.0 data/cyc10_d.npy`
  4. `python3 code/cert.py 10 data/cyc10_d.npy data/c10`
  5. Select the roots with unimod == "unimodular" in `c10_cert.json` and sort them lexicographically by phase. This
     gives `bu10_x.npy` and `bu10_phases.npy`.
  6. `python3 code/cert.py 10 data/bu10_x.npy data/bu10hp 256 5 1e-45`
  7. `python3 code/analyse.py 10 data/bu10_phases.npy data/d10`
  8. `python3 code/rigor.py 10 data/bu10_x.npy data/d10_analysis.json data/d10`
  9. `python3 code/classify.py`
  10. `python3 code/exact_cyclo.py`
- The core chain (steps 1–8) takes under 10 minutes on one core.
